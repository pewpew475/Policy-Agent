================================

12.1 ACCIDENT PROCEDURES
If you're involved in an accident:
1. Ensure everyone's safety and call 911 if anyone is injured
2. Move vehicles to safety if possible
3. Exchange information with other drivers
4. Take photos of damage and accident scene
5. Get contact information from witnesses
6. Call police if required by law or if there are injuries
7. Contact our claims department within 24 hours

1. Call police immediately and file a report
2. Contact our claims department within 24 hours
3. Provide police report number
4. List any personal property that was in the vehicle
5. Provide all vehicle keys to the adjuster
6. Cooperate with the investigation

For weather-related damage:
1. Document damage with photos
2. Make temporary repairs to prevent further damage
3. Keep receipts for emergency repairs
4. Contact claims department as soon as safely possible
5. Do not make permanent repairs until adjuster inspection

SECTION 13: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
======================================

13.1 COVERAGE QUESTIONS

Q: Does my policy cover rental cars?
A: Yes, if you have rental car coverage. You're covered up to $50 per day for 30 days when your vehicle is being repaired due to a covered claim.

Q: Am I covered if I lend my car to someone else?
A: Yes, coverage follows the vehicle. However, the other driver must have a valid license and your permission to drive.

Q: What if I'm in an accident in another state?
A: You're covered anywhere in the United States and Canada. Your policy meets the minimum requirements of all states.

Q: Does my policy cover business use?
A: Personal auto policies do not cover commercial use. If you use your vehicle for business, you need commercial coverage.

13.2 CLAIMS QUESTIONS

Q: How long do I have to report a claim?
A: You must report claims within 30 days of the incident, but it's best to report as soon as possible.

Q: Can I choose my own repair shop?
A: Yes, you can choose any licensed repair facility. We also have a network of preferred shops that guarantee their work.

Q: What if the other driver doesn't have insurance?
A: Your uninsured motorist coverage will protect you. This coverage pays for injuries and property damage caused by uninsured drivers.

Q: How is my deductible applied?
A: You pay the deductible amount directly to the repair shop or service provider. We pay the remaining covered amount.

13.3 POLICY QUESTIONS

Q: Can I cancel my policy at any time?
A: Yes, you can cancel at any time. If you cancel mid-term, you'll receive a prorated refund of unused premium.

Q: What happens if I miss a payment?
A: You have a 10-day grace period. After that, a $25 late fee applies. If payment is 30 days late, your policy may be cancelled.

Q: How often does my rate change?
A: Rates are typically reviewed annually at renewal. Changes may occur due to claims, violations, credit changes, or rate adjustments.

APPENDIX A: DEFINITIONS
=======================

Actual Cash Value: The cost to replace property with property of like kind and quality, less depreciation.

Bodily Injury: Physical injury, sickness, or disease sustained by a person, including death resulting from any of these.

Collision: The upset or impact of a covered auto with another vehicle or object.

Comprehensive Coverage: Coverage for loss to your covered auto caused by other than collision, such as fire, theft, vandalism, or hitting an animal.

Deductible: The amount you must pay before your insurance coverage applies to a claim.

Liability: Legal responsibility for damages or injuries caused to others.

Premium: The amount you pay for your insurance coverage.

Property Damage: Physical damage to or destruction of tangible property, including loss of use of that property.

Total Loss: When the cost to repair a vehicle exceeds its actual cash value, or when a vehicle is stolen and not recovered.

Uninsured Motorist: A driver who does not have liability insurance, has insufficient coverage, or whose insurance company denies coverage or becomes insolvent.
